**Title: The Big Screen Busts: Biggest Box Office Flops of 2024**

Every year, Hollywood churns out a plethora of films, each vying for the audience's attention and, of course, box office glory. But not all movies manage to hit the mark. In 2024, several big-budget films found themselves on the wrong side of the ledger, becoming some of the year's most talked-about flops. These films serve as a reminder that even with star power and hefty budgets, success is never guaranteed.

### 1. Kraven the Hunter: A Superhero Misstep

Sony Pictures' "Kraven the Hunter" was anticipated to be a thrilling addition to the superhero genre. However, the film stumbled right out of the gate. With a budget that ballooned to $110 million due to reshoots, the movie only managed to rake in $62 million worldwide. The cultural shift away from superhero flicks, coupled with a poor C CinemaScore, meant that Kraven was left in the dust ([source](https://www.boxofficemojo.com/)).

### 2. Megalopolis: A Vision Too Grand

Francis Ford Coppola's "Megalopolis" was a film that promised grandeur but unfortunately delivered underwhelming results. Despite Coppola's personal investment and a budget of $120 million, the film's critical reception was lukewarm at best, scoring a mere 45% on Rotten Tomatoes. The use of AI-generated marketing quotes didn't help, as the film opened to just $4 million domestically ([source](https://www.rottentomatoes.com/)).

### 3. Borderlands: Lost in Translation

Based on the popular video game, "Borderlands" had a fan base ready to embrace its cinematic counterpart. However, costly reshoots and a delayed release date meant that this $120 million project only saw $8.6 million in its opening weekend. The movie's style failed to resonate with both fans and new audiences alike ([source](https://www.forbes.com/)).

### 4. Furiosa: A Mad Max Saga's Deserted Journey

The much-anticipated "Furiosa: A Mad Max Saga" struggled to find its footing despite positive reviews. With a budget of $168 million, the absence of Charlize Theron and mixed marketing messages led to a net loss of nearly $120 million. Even with a B+ CinemaScore, the film couldn't capture the magic of its predecessors ([source](https://www.variety.com/)).

### 5. Joker: Folie à Deux: A Sequel's Sobering Tune

Riding on the coattails of its predecessor, "Joker: Folie à Deux" aimed high but fell short. The film's shift towards a musical tone didn't resonate well, resulting in a disappointing D CinemaScore. With a staggering budget of $200 million, the film became the year's biggest financial letdown with a net loss of $144.25 million ([source](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/)).

These films, despite their potential, highlight the unpredictable nature of the film industry. From changes in audience preferences to marketing missteps, the path to box office success is fraught with challenges.
